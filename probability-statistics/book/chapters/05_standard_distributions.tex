\chapter{A library of chance mechanisms}
\chaptertag{Standard discrete and continuous distributions}

\begin{question}
Which probability laws recur because the same mechanisms recur?
\end{question}

\section{Discrete families}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{lXll}
\toprule
Law & Mechanism and mass function & Mean & Variance \\
\midrule
Bernoulli$(p)$ & one success indicator; $p^x(1-p)^{1-x}$ & $p$ & $p(1-p)$ \\
Binomial$(n,p)$ & successes in $n$ independent trials; $\binom nkp^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ & $np$ & $np(1-p)$ \\
Geometric$(p)$ & trials through first success; $(1-p)^{k-1}p$ & $1/p$ & $(1-p)/p^2$ \\
Poisson$(\lambda)$ & rare-event count; $e^{-\lambda}\lambda^k/k!$ & $\lambda$ & $\lambda$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The geometric law is memoryless:
\[
\P(X>s+t\mid X>s)=\P(X>t).
\]
The Poisson law arises as a limit of $\Bin(n,p)$ when $n$ is large, $p$ is small, and $np\approx\lambda$.

\section{Continuous families}

Uniform$(a,b)$ assigns constant density $1/(b-a)$ to an interval. Exponential$(\lambda)$ has density $\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$ on $x\ge0$ and is the continuous memoryless law.

The normal density with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$ is
\[
f(x)=\frac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right).
\]
Standardization sends $X\sim\Normal(\mu,\sigma^2)$ to
\[
Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}\sim\Normal(0,1).
\]

The beta family on $[0,1]$ has density proportional to $x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1}$. It is useful for proportions and becomes a natural prior for a Bernoulli probability.

\section{Choose by mechanism, not by silhouette}

A bell-shaped histogram does not prove normality. Ask what generated the observations, what values are possible, whether trials are independent, whether rates are stable, and what approximation error is acceptable.

\begin{exercisebox}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Compute $\P(X\ge2)$ for $X\sim\Bin(5,.3)$. \exlevel{Fluency}
  \item Prove the geometric memoryless property. \exlevel{Reasoning}
  \item Approximate $\Bin(1000,.002)$ by a Poisson law and compare $\P(X=0)$. \exlevel{Exploration}
  \item Find the median of an exponential distribution. \exlevel{Fluency}
  \item If $X\sim\Normal(10,9)$, express $\P(8<X<13)$ using the standard normal CDF. \exlevel{Fluency}
  \item Derive the mean and variance of Uniform$(a,b)$. \exlevel{Reasoning}
  \item For each family in the chapter, invent one plausible and one implausible real-world use. \exlevel{Exploration}
\end{enumerate}
\end{exercisebox}
